# Digi Night 17z33: what's new and what to try

## What changed

**Four modes, and a new main menu**
- The menu now opens on four big mode cards: **Story**, **Survival**, **Night Siege** and **Versus**. Hatchery, Eggs and Discoveries sit in a wider row underneath, so they don't look like they belong to the card above them.
- **No more Online tile.** Pick any mode, then on the setup screen choose **Just play** or **Invite friends**. Invite friends opens the lobby with that mode already picked. Got an invite yourself? **Join a friend** is up top, next to your DigiCoins.
- **Story (solo or co-op):** Jijimon gives you one goal at a time: hatch, beat wild Digimon, feed your partner, reach job level 5, learn a move, digivolve, pick a side, visit the farm, reach Rookie, beat a boss, reach Champion.
  - The goal and how far you've got show at the top of the screen. Each goal pays a bit of XP.
  - The game opens up as you go. The rival shows up at the Rookie goal, virus outbreaks at the boss goal, and world events after the last goal. Tokomon waits to ask about sides until that's the goal.
  - **With friends, the party shares the goals.** "Beat wild Digimon" counts everyone's kills (5 each). Goals like "Feed your partner" need every player to do them, and the top bar shows "waiting on your team" once you're done.
  - A fallen partner hatches again, like Open World. After the last goal, the run carries on with everything on.
- **Survival:** the whole open world, same as before. Its four rule sets (Open World/Respawn, One Life, Ranked, Weekly Map) are picked on the setup screen. Only Survival runs go on the League boards.
- **Night Siege (solo or co-op, one life):**
  - **By day:** level up and stock up.
  - **At dusk:** you get a warning. Then waves come at every player from off screen until dawn.
  - **Each night is harder:** more attackers and stronger stages. Every 3rd night brings a boss.
  - **At dawn:** the attackers left run off, and everyone still standing gets XP and bits.
  - **Score:** the top bar shows the time to night or dawn and how many attackers are left. The game over screen counts the nights you held out.
- **Versus (online only, two or more players):**
  - **The race:** 10 minutes, with each player starting on their own side of the map. The top bar shows the clock and both levels.
  - **The middle of the map:** an XP cache drops every minute, and a mini boss shows up at 2:30, 5:00 and 7:30. They're marked on the minimap.
  - **Falling in the race:** your partner comes back on your side of the map, same form and level.
  - **The showdown:** everyone is pulled into an arena and the wild Digimon are cleared. Then it's best of three rounds with a 3-2-1 countdown, and tamers can't be hurt.
  - **Round rules:** a knocked-out partner is out of that round, not sent to the farm. A round that runs past 75 seconds goes to whoever has more health left.
  - **The win:** a "You win!" or "You lose" card, then the game over screen.
  - **Hosting:** clicking Versus on the menu hosts a room. The lobby won't start Versus until someone has joined.
- **What it has eaten (Hatchery).** Under the Digimon on the stand: a bar split by data type, with each type's name, amount and share, for what it has eaten **since it last grew** (what steers its next digivolution, or which egg it hatches into) and for **all it has eaten** (what goes with it into a run). Names and numbers as well as colours.
- **Three Hatchery slots, and death matters.**
  - **Three slots:** raise up to three Digimon at once. Each slot has its own egg and its **own bloodline**. Your data is still one pool and you choose where it goes: pour it all into one, or share it out. Tap a slot to put it **on the stand**: that's the one you feed, take into a run from the setup screen, and bring to a Duel. Each slot card shows what's in it, its line and generation, and how many traits it has. A save from before keeps its Digimon and line in slot 1.
  - **When your Hatchery Digimon falls in its run** (the run ends because your partner fell, or in Open World the first time it falls and starts over as an egg):
    - **It had reached Mega:** a **valorous death**. The line goes on and earns its 2 traits as usual.
    - **As an Ultimate:** the line goes on but loses 1 trait level (the biggest first), and earns nothing.
    - **As a Champion (or Armor):** the line loses 3 trait levels, and earns nothing.
    - **As a Rookie or younger:** the whole bloodline is **erased**, traits and all, and that slot starts a new line. The Hatchery remembers which line ended and why.
  - **A run it survives** earns traits as before (Mega 2, a great run 1). **In Night Siege, losing the nest counts as a fall** (judged by its stage the same way), since guarding the eggs is the job. Your tamer going down isn't your Digimon dying.
  - The run-over screen says what happened to the line, and the family tree marks valorous deaths (gold) and falls (red) with what was lost.
  - **Expeditions go as a team:** everything hatched in your slots goes out together (not the one in your run): the best one's finds, +15% for each other one, with the data shared out over their elements.

- **Expeditions (Hatchery).** The Digimon on your Hatchery stand (once it has hatched) goes exploring whenever you play a run without it, and comes back when the run ends, so even a run that goes badly brings something home.
  - **What it finds**, for each minute you were out (up to 30): data of its own element (4 a minute for a Rookie) and DigiCoins (3 a minute). A Baby finds half as much, an In-Training three quarters, a Champion 1.4x, and an older bloodline up to half again more (+5% a generation).
  - **Sometimes more:** an item for your stash (about 3% a minute, up to 50% a run) and, rarely, a **map to a rare egg** (about 1.2% a minute, up to 30%): your next new egg is Rare or better.
  - The run-over screen says what it brought back, and the Hatchery shows its last trip under the stand.
  - Nothing for a run shorter than a minute, a Duel, or a run you took it into (then it was with you).

- **A League board for every mode.** The League window (LEAGUE on the main menu; it needs you logged in on the online server, like before) has four new tabs next to the Survival ones:
  - **Story:** fastest to finish the story.
  - **Night Siege:** most nights held in one run.
  - **Versus:** Versus matches won, all time.
  - **Duel:** no ranking. Duels are just for fun, so this tab only shows **your own record**: wins, losses and the share won. Every finished duel, won or lost, goes on it. Duels still never level, digivolve, earn traits or pay anything.
  - **Every entry shows what it was done with:** the partner's form, and "Hatchery Gen N" if it came from your Hatchery (a Hatchery run, or the Digimon you duelled with).
  - Each tab says in a line what it counts. Survival's boards (Level, Fastest Mega, Survival, Lords, PvP) now only count Survival runs, so a long Night Siege run doesn't land on the Survival board.
  - What sends a run in: a Survival run of a minute or more (as before), a finished Story, a Night Siege run with a night held, a won Versus or Duel. The server turns away runs that couldn't be real (9 nights in 5 minutes, a Versus win before the race was over).

**Spectating, and world events made for friends**
- **Spectating:** online, when you're down in a one-life run and waiting for a teammate to pick you up, the camera now follows a friend who's still fighting.
  - A bar along the bottom shows who you're watching, their Digimon, level and health.
  - **Left / right** (keys, stick, the joystick, or the arrows on the bar) flick between everyone still up. The last stop is **your own spot**, where a friend has to come to revive you.
  - If the friend you're watching goes down too, it moves to the next one. Once you're revived, the camera is back on you.
- **World events with friends:** the three world events (virus outbreak, Dark Area surge, rampaging Mega) were already in the game, but rare. Most runs had none, and they came 8 to 50 minutes in.
  - **Alone:** still rare, as before.
  - **With 2 or more tamers in the run:** the first one comes **5 to 10 minutes** after you're together, then another **15 to 25 minutes** after each one ends.
  - **Crew bonus:** every extra tamer who turns up to fight it adds **+20% pay for everyone**, up to +60%. It shows in the victory message.

**Sharp zoom**
- The mouse wheel used to zoom in small free steps (70% to 180%), so the camera almost never sat where the pixel art is crisp, and the ground looked a little smeared.
- Now **each notch jumps to the next sharp zoom**. Pixel art is crisp when each art pixel covers a whole number of your screen's pixels. On a normal screen that's **100% and 200%**; on a 150% Windows screen, 133% and 200%; on a phone or Retina screen, 100%, 150% and 200%. There's also one **wide view** (about 70%) for seeing more, which is a little soft, and the zoom note says so.
- A pinch on a phone snaps to the nearest step when you let go. A small note shows the zoom when it changes. The closest zoom in went from 180% to 200%.
- The ground, trees, rocks and your tamer are pixel-perfect at those steps. Digimon sprites are each scaled to their own size, so they look smooth at any zoom but are never exactly on the grid.

**Equipped moves, a move bar, and the Move Slot**
- **Your partner only uses the moves it has equipped:** 2 as a Baby or In-Training, 3 as a Rookie, and 4 from Champion up. It can still learn as many as you like.
- **Swap any time** in Skills (K). Every learned move has an **Equip** or **Unequip** button, and the window shows your equipped moves at the top. With every slot full, pressing Equip asks which one to swap out. You can't unequip your last move.
- A move learned with every slot full waits in the skills window, and a message tells you. A bigger move of the same kind (Nova Blast after Pepper Breath) takes its smaller one's slot by itself. New slots from digivolving fill up on their own. A move you take off by hand stays off.
- **A bigger move needs the one before it at Lv 7** (it was Lv 3). Every move still goes to Lv 10.
- **The move bar:** bottom right (along the bottom, left of the buttons, on phones). There's a square for each slot with the move's emblem, name and level. A ready move is in full colour with a gold border. When it fires, it turns grey and a dark sweep winds back like a clock as it recharges. Empty slots are dashed. Orbits never wait, so they always show ready.
- **Move Slot** in the Lab: 1,500 DigiCoins, one level only. It gives one more equipped move at every stage: 3 as a Baby, 4 as a Rookie, 5 from Champion.
- **Balance:** a partner used to fire every move it knew, 6 to 16 of them by Champion. With only 3 or 4 now, **each equipped move hits 1.75 times as hard**. Smaller moves in a chain also add more to the bigger one (3% per level, was 1.5%), so climbing a chain pays off. Measured against Night Siege bosses, nights 3 to 12 come out about the same as before, and night 18 at about 80%.
- CPU partners, rivals and Duel builds now fill their slots first, then raise the move that unlocks a bigger one.
- **Fixed for online guests:** spending a skill point, raising a stat and swapping moves sometimes showed "Can't learn that yet" even though it worked. Guests now see the right result straight away.
- Story's skill goal, the tour, How to Play and the skills window all explain equipped moves and the move bar.

**Story mode now teaches the whole game**
- Jijimon's goals went from 11 to 18. The new ones: **Raise a stat (T)**, **Train at the farm**, **Hit a weakness** (land 3 hits your foe is weak to) and **Warp digivolve (R)**. The old "Go back to the farm" goal is now the training one, since you have to go to the farm to train.
- His words now explain more as you go: CRIT and MISS on the damage numbers, the two levels (job level gives skill points, base level gives stat points, and what every stat does), the stages up to Mega, the up and down arrows for weak and resist, and the warp meter.
- After the Champion goal come **four short lessons** about what's waiting outside the run, each on screen for about 15 seconds: the Hatchery, bloodlines and expeditions, the other modes (Survival, Night Siege, Versus, Duel) and the League. Lessons pay no XP and need nothing from you. Then the story is done, and the run goes on.
- **Missed what Jijimon said? Click the goal chip at the top** and he says it again.

**The tutorial is up to date**
- Jijimon's first-time tour no longer mentions things that are gone. It now covers the four modes (endless Night Siege with fences, Versus and Duel), the three Hatchery slots, bloodlines and expeditions, a League board for every mode, and the two kinds of level.
- The setup screen part has two new stops: **What you bring** (your Crest Piece, Charm and one-run items) and **With friends** (Invite friends instead of Just play). There's a new card on **job level and base level**.
- **How to Play** has new sections: Four ways to play, Two kinds of level, Damage numbers, the League, Hatchery slots and bloodlines (every death rule), and Expeditions. The old Hatchery line that said it "does not come back" now explains bloodlines.

**The League got a glow-up**
- It now fills the whole screen: drifting stars and sweeping light beams behind, a shimmering rainbow **LEAGUE** title with the week under it, and a gold frame that pulses.
- **Emblem tabs:** every board is a chunky button with its own medal, grouped by mode (Survival gold, Story mint, Night Siege violet, Versus red, Duel orange). The board you're on lifts up and glows.
- **A podium for the top three:** their Digimon stand on gold, silver and bronze blocks, breathing and bobbing, with big **1ST / 2ND / 3RD** numbers. The winner gets a pulsing crown. Each shows the player, their score, and their form and Hatchery generation.
- **Your banner:** "YOU #4", or "On the podium!", or how to get on the board if you aren't yet.
- Places 4 to 20 slide in one after another; your own row is highlighted and marked.
- **Duel** shows your record as a big glowing ring with the share you've won.
- **Faction season** is now a tug-of-war: one bar split between the biomes by land taken, each piece named, and the leader marked with ▲ in the list below.
- Places are always numbers and words too, never just gold, silver or bronze. With reduced effects on, nothing moves.

- **Duel (new, under Versus).** In the lobby, Versus now has two kinds: **Race** (the 7-minute race to Mega) and **Duel**.
  - **Straight into the arena** with your Hatchery Digimon just as it is: its form, its job level for its stage (Rookie 8, Champion 14), and its **bloodline traits**. No race, no levelling, no digivolving. Best of three; with three or more it's every partner for themselves, as in the Versus showdown.
  - **Nothing to lose:** your Hatchery Digimon isn't spent and nothing about it changes. A duel doesn't pay rank XP, DigiBank memories or Hatchery data either, so it can't be farmed. The end screen says "Your Hatchery Digimon is just as it was."
  - Its skill points are spent for it the way you'd build it: its own main move first, then its melee, then the rest.
  - **The lobby** shows YOUR HATCHERY DIGIMON in place of the egg (name, level, generation, its traits), and everyone's card shows what they're bringing. Nothing hatched yet? You fight as Agumon, Lv 8, and the lobby and the arena tell you so.
  - Checked online: the guest brought a Garurumon (Gen 3, INT +4, Power +4%), the host fell back to Agumon, and the duel ran to the end screen.

- **Bloodlines (Hatchery).** Your Hatchery Digimon are one line now, parent to child, still one at a time. Each one you take into a run is a generation.
  - **Earning traits:** if it **reaches Mega** in its run, the line earns **2 traits**; a **great run** earns **1**: reaching Ultimate, lasting 20 minutes, holding 6 nights in Night Siege, or finishing the story. The run-over screen tells you what it passed on.
  - **Every egg after carries all of them** into its run. Traits: a stat (+2 STR, AGI, VIT, INT, DEX or LUK a level, mostly the one its element favours, sometimes the one that goes with it), **Power** (+4% damage a level), **Quick Study** (+6% XP), **Head Start** (+1 skill point at the start), **Lucky Scales** (better shiny chance). Each goes to level 5, and a line holds 16 levels in all.
  - **The Hatchery shows the line:** its generation, each trait as words and numbers with its level, and a **Family tree** button listing every generation: what it was, what it became, why it passed something on (or didn't) and what it carried. The status window (T) says when a stat came from the bloodline.
- **Night Siege bosses: health that makes sense, and they don't leave.**
  - A boss's health is now Night Siege's own instead of the open world's (which ran into the millions): **6,000 on night 3**, then **30% more each night**: about 13,000 on night 6, 29,000 on night 9, 64,000 on night 12. That's about 50 seconds for a partner built for that night. A **giant** is 1.6x the night's boss (more each time round the special nights). With two tamers a boss has 60% more, and 60% more again for each extra tamer.
  - **More tamers, more and tougher attackers (Night Siege only):** for each tamer past the first, waves are 40% bigger (two tamers 1.4x, three 1.8x, four 2.2x, and the most that can be out at once grows the same way) and every ordinary attacker has 20% more health. This replaces the old "one more attacker per extra tamer".
  - **A boss stays past dawn.** The other attackers still run off, but a boss or giant you didn't beat stays, day and all, still going for the eggs, until you beat it. The dawn card tells you who stayed.
- **A Mega's main move hits harder.** A Mega's own signature is its ultimate (R), so it fights with its Ultimate's main move; now, at Mega, that main move (its whole primary line) does 1.6x. In the bench, WarGreymon's best hit went from 621 to 860 and its damage a second from about 3,000 to 4,000, against MetalGreymon's 638 and 1,850. Its average hit still looks close to MetalGreymon's because a Mega also fires lots of small extra moves (aura, sweeps, stomps) that pull the average down.
- **Checked: your damage does grow.** A partner that puts its skill points into its own main move and its status points into its main stat goes from about 42 a hit (Agumon, night 3) to 140-270 a hit with far more hits a second later on: about 117 damage a second on night 3, 265 on night 6, 580 on night 9 and 1,850 on night 12. Points left unspent or spread over every move keep a partner hitting for single digits. (scripts/siege-boss-bench.js measures it.)

- **Damage numbers on every hit, in every mode (Ragnarok style).**
  - **Every single hit gets its own number**, never added together, so you see exactly what each hit did. Hits landing on the same enemy at the same moment fan out left and right so none hides another. Up to 140 can be on screen (48 with reduced effects), so crowds don't lose them.
  - **Criticals:** bigger, gold, with "!" and a small **CRIT** over the number.
  - **Weakness:** orange with an **up arrow**. **Resisted:** grey with a **down arrow**. The arrows mean you can tell them apart without colour.
  - **PvP and Versus showdowns:** hits on a rival's partner now show numbers too.
  - Lightning striking a puddle now shows numbers for everything it zaps.
  - **Every miss says MISS**, in the same pixel letters as the numbers (pale, smaller, drifting up off the target), not the old small plain-text "miss". Several misses at one spot fan out like the numbers. Online, everyone sees every miss.
  - **No numbers on your own partner** when it gets hit: the red edge and the jolt on a big hit are still there. That red edge now only shows for your own partner, not when a friend's gets hit online.
  - Online, guests now see up to 32 hits a snapshot (was 10), so they get nearly every number too.

- **Versus: race to Mega (new).**
  - **7-minute race** (was 10). The mini bosses come at 1:45, 3:30 and 5:15.
  - **You start as a Rookie.** In the lobby, when the host picks Versus, the egg picker becomes **YOUR ROOKIE**: Agumon, Gabumon and Veemon are always there, plus any Rookie you've raised in the **Hatchery** (Rookies reached in a run don't count). The Hatchery now remembers every Rookie it raises; one raised before this update only counts if it's the egg on the stand now. Your friends see your pick on your card. Every Rookie offered has a line that goes to Mega.
  - **XP and level-ups are the same as ever**, so the race isn't spent clicking skill cards. What's quicker is digivolving.
  - **The DIGIVOLVE meter** sits in the top bar next to your level, with a number beside it, and shows your rival's too. When it's full and your partner has the level (4 for Champion, 7 for Ultimate, 10 for Mega), it digivolves on the spot along its line, and the meter starts again. If it's full but you're short on level, it says "full · needs Lv 7".
    - **Kills** fill it a little: 1 a kill, 3 for an elite, 8 for a boss.
    - **Caches** in the middle now also drop 6 orange DIGIVOLVE orbs (a double arrow pointing up), worth 5 each.
    - **Mini bosses** pay everyone who hurt them, by how much damage each did: 60, 60, then 80 for the last one. Criticals and hitting its weakness count, so a smaller partner with the right element can earn more than a bigger one. You see "+N DIGIVOLVE" and a toast with your share.
    - **No runaway leader:** whoever is clearly ahead (by a quarter of a meter or more, counting stages) gets 75% of what the middle pays. Kills always pay in full.
  - **The target marker is much easier to see:** a thick ring on the ground round the targeted partner with four brackets turning on it, a big arrow bobbing over its head pointing down, and TARGET or YOUR TARGET in a dark box above. **Fixed:** for guests it never showed at all (the host has a different name on a guest's screen); now it does, checked with three players.
  - **Fixed:** in Versus your XP was being shared with your rival, as in co-op. Now it's yours alone.
  - **Three or more in the showdown: gang up on the leader.** It's every partner for themselves. Tap or click a rival's partner to send yours after it; that pick holds until it's knocked out or the round ends. Until you pick, your partner goes for the strongest one still standing (stage, then level, then health), so the Champion and the Ultimate both go for the Mega, then turn on each other once it's out. The top bar says who you're after and whether it's your pick or the strongest, and a ring with TARGET (or YOUR TARGET) sits over that partner. The showdown card explains it when there are three or more of you. A match of two works as before.
  - Checked online: the guest picked Gabumon in the lobby, the host saw it on their card, and both started as their Rookies with their own meters.
- **Setup screen, bottom strip:** the "Isaac as Cyan, with the Agumon Line egg…" line is gone. In its place is a bigger **Bringing in** panel with a socket for every loadout slot: Crest (and a second Crest slot, shown locked until you buy the Lab's Digivice Port), Charm, and your one-run item or scroll. Each socket shows the item's emblem and name, or an empty socket. Tap it to change your items. The only text left there is a warning when something stops you starting, like a locked egg.
- **Your name, not your colour:** your profile name now shows under your tamer in game. The lobby player list shows each player's name and egg, without the projection colour. The colour names stay on the projection picker.
- **New holograms:** your projection has no legs now. It's redrawn with half again as many pixels (finer outlines, a rim light, shading, glinting eyes and fine scanlines). Below the waist it breaks apart into digital mist: the rows slip and the pixels drop out over the ring it's projected from.
  - **Moving:** it glides and floats instead of walking. It leans into the move, the mist streams out behind it, and a faint afterimage trails for a moment.
  - **Below the waist** you still sense a body coming apart, so it never looks like a floating torso. A faint column of flickering scanlines tapers down to the ring, bright glitch slices slide across it, and about 30 sparkles drift down and spread (a few twinkle white). Moving, it all streams out behind.
  - **Size:** in game it stands a little taller than before, since the body now gets the room the legs had. In the menus it's fitted to the old size.
- **Chat (online):**
  - **In the lobby:** a chat box sits under the player list. Press Enter to start typing, Enter to send and Esc to stop.
  - **In a run:** Enter opens a "Say:" line at the bottom left. Everyone in the run sees your message there, however far apart you are. Lines fade after about 9 seconds, and all recent ones show while you're typing.
  - **While you type:** your tamer stops, because the keys are typing. Enter sends and gives you control back, and Esc cancels. A controller still moves you.
  - **On a touch screen:** there's a small 💬 button.
  - **The rules:** messages go through the host, which caps them at 140 characters and allows 5 every 6 seconds per player. Someone who joins late gets the last 20 lobby lines.
- **A secret projection:** the black hologram with the cigarette (a thank-you to one of the testers).
  - **Hidden:** he isn't on the picker until he's unlocked.
  - **Unlocking:** type the code in the online lobby chat, starting with "/". The code is never sent to anyone, and only you see the "Unlocked" note.
  - **After that:** he stays on that device's projection picker for good, and everyone else in the run sees him.
  - **Other codes:** a wrong code just says "Nothing happens." A code typed in game says codes only work in the lobby.
- **Feeding, hunger and happiness:**
  - **Why feeding sometimes stopped:** a partner that had starved once and was a bit unhappy would quietly refuse food once its belly was over 15%. It wasn't a cooldown, and nothing told you why. Now a hungry partner always eats. It only sulks when it's more than 45% full, and then it says so ("Sulking: not hungry enough to give in"). A partner that's nearly full (96%) says "It's full!".
  - **Hunger is a need:** below 35% a food bubble pops up over your partner's head (other players' too). At 10% or less the bubble turns red and pulses, and your partner is starving: 20% slower, 15% weaker, and it gets unhappier by the second.
  - **Happiness is how you treat it:** happiness goes up for fight streaks, beating bosses (+8), level ups (+3), its favourite food (+12) and resting somewhere quiet. Resting means standing still, fed, with no wild Digimon nearby, and it goes up to 85. Happiness drops when it starves, faints (−12) or takes big hits. Ordinary hits barely matter now, where before every scratch drained it.
  - **Food:** meat, fish and veg mostly fill it up, and berries (sweets) mostly cheer it up. Each Digimon has a favourite food, marked with a ♥ in the feed menu, and tastes can change when it digivolves.
- **The farm is a place now:**
  - **Getting there:** the Farm button (G) and its countdown are gone. A glowing Digi-gate (a pixel archway with a little fence and a FARM sign) stands just north of where every run starts. Another appears beside every nest your side has taken over, coloured like that biome. Walk up to one and press E (Y on a controller, Act on a phone). You still can't go in with an enemy close.
  - **Finding it:** the Farm chip on the HUD shows the way to the nearest gate with an arrow and a step count, and it's marked on the minimap.
  - **Feeding is at the farm only.** Food you find goes in your bag, and you feed your partner at the farm. Pressing F out in the world tells you so. The belly drains much slower now: a full one lasts about 8 minutes, up from about 3, so you head home now and then rather than all the time.
  - **Story mode** walks you to the gate for the feeding goal.
  - **F does nothing out in the world now.** It only opens the feed menu at the farm.
- **Farm training is worth more, and costs more:**
  - **Food is stamina:** every session costs a quarter of the belly (the play station costs a tenth), and your partner won't train when it's under a third full.
  - **Meals by taste:** its favourite food fills a third of the belly (♥ in the feed menu), ordinary food a quarter, and food it dislikes only a sixth and makes it a little grumpy (☹ in the feed menu). Each Digimon has one food it dislikes, and that can change when it digivolves.
  - **A smaller bag:** you can carry at most 10 pieces of food. The bag counter shows how full it is, and with a full bag, food stays on the ground.
  - **Fresh sessions:** you start with 3 fresh sessions, which pay in full. After that each session pays less (70%, 45%, 25%) until you go out and fight. Every 10 wild Digimon you beat brings back one fresh session, up to 3. Walking out and straight back in doesn't reset anything.
  - **Gates make it count:** every nest gate your side holds adds 10% to what training gives, up to double. The start gate doesn't count.
  - **On the farm panel:** it shows the gate bonus, your fresh sessions (and how many more wild Digimon you need for the next one) and the session cost. After each session you see what you gained, the gate bonus, and how tired your partner was.
- **Black spires are a gamble now:** every time you break one, a hidden roll decides what comes out:
  - **About 70%:** a swarm of 5–6 In-Training Digimon. Easy experience.
  - **About 20%:** a pack of 3 In-Training ones led by 1–2 Rookies.
  - **About 10%:** first the ground shakes ("Something huge is coming… RUN!"), and about 1.6 seconds later an evil mini boss, well above you, climbs out. It's faster than normal and hunts you down.
  - **Chests:** beat everything the spire let out and a chest drops where the last one fell. It's bronze for the swarm, silver for the pack and gold for the mini boss. Walk over it to open it.

  - **The boost:** a partner that turns shiny digivolving gets +2.5% to everything: damage, health, speed and attack rate. One that hatched shiny gets +5%. It keeps the boost for the rest of the run, through every form.
  - **The shiny moment:** when your partner turns shiny, only your screen plays a 3-second event. Big golden-rainbow "SHINY!" letters pop in over spinning light rays and twinkling sparkles, with a line saying how much stronger it is. Nothing can hurt you or your partner for those 3 seconds. Playing alone, the game holds still for it; online, the world keeps going for everyone else, but you're safe. The pause screen tag shows the boost afterwards (✦ Shiny +5%).
  - **Every Baby and In-Training form has a shiny look**, made with the same colour formula as the rest. Mostly-black ones like Botamon now get a coloured body too, not just new eyes.
- **Online lobby:** the host picks one of the four modes (and the Survival rules). Friends now play the host's mode (before, a friend always counted as Open World).


**Fixes**
- **Blur and lag next to growing snow (or any biome war):** fixed at the root. Host frame spikes went from about 280 ms to 100 ms in the same test.

**Signature moves**
- Every Digimon line has its own signature moves:
  - 2 at Baby and at In-Training, about 4 at Rookie and Champion, more at Ultimate and Mega.
  - Moves from earlier stages carry over.
  - Some moves grow into a stronger version and replace the old one.
  - The Rookie branch choice locks out the other path for the run.
- Difficulty is a smidge harder than before.
- **Every move looks different now.** All 364 have their own shape, colours, motion, trail and hit effect, and I've hand-checked them all.
- Upgraded moves look like a bigger version of the move they replaced.
- **New move emblems:** the level-up cards and skill tree show a detailed, shaded emblem for every move, each one different, built from the move's own look.
- **Gallery:** open `/moves-gallery.html` to see every line's moves side by side. Click a box to replay a move; there's a slow-motion button.

**Turf wars and sides**
- Tokomon pops up early and asks you to pick a side, or "Just survive". Pause > Your side changes it later.
- **Only one friendly biome at a time.** If you don't pick, every biome you help warms up to you. The first one you win over all the way (to "Trusted") becomes your side, and the others go back to neutral.
- **Victory:** win when your side holds half the land around the start. That earns new badges and extra rank pay.

**World events (rare)**
- **How often:** most runs have none, some have one, and now and then a run has two. They come 8 to 50 minutes in.
- **The three kinds:**
  - A virus outbreak: hunt down Patient Zero.
  - A Dark Area surge: the land turns to Glitch until you beat the Surge Lord.
  - A rampaging Mega.
- **Warnings:** everyone gets a warning, then a timer at the top of the screen and a marker on the minimap.
- **Rewards:** when it falls, everyone gets paid, whatever side they're on. There are 5 new badges.

**Picking an egg**
- On the run setup, click the egg (or **See all eggs**) for a grid of every egg. Opened ones show the egg, the baby it hatches into, how it plays (like "Fire · up close") and a few moves; unopened ones are silhouettes. Hover one to see the whole line and every digivolution path you've reached with it. The online lobby (tap the egg's name) and Your Digi Eggs screen have it too.
